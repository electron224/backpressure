# Rate Limiting

A rate limiter sits in front of a service and decides, per request, whether it may pass. Over the limit, requests get a fast 429 instead of a slow timeout.

## Why it exists

An unprotected service under overload queues until p99 explodes and everything times out together. A limiter sheds excess load early: the service stays fast for admitted requests, and callers get a clear retryable signal instead of silence.

## The algorithms

- **Token bucket** refills continuously up to a burst capacity. Short bursts pass; sustained overload sheds smoothly. The burst size is how much sudden spike you forgive. (The lab's burst slider only affects token bucket; sliding-window ignores it.)
- **Sliding window** counts requests in fixed windows. Simple, but a burst at the end of one window plus a burst at the start of the next admits nearly twice the limit across the edge — the boundary burst. With a 100 RPS window, 100 requests at :999 and 100 at :1000 all pass.
- **Leaky bucket** is token bucket's mirror: requests queue and drain at a fixed rate. Smooth outflow instead of bursty inflow. Same math family, different shape.

## The failure mode to remember

Watch 429s, not latency. A healthy limiter shows high rejects with a clean service behind it. No rejects under overload means the limiter is misconfigured or missing — the queue is absorbing what should have been shed.

## Interview line

"Limit per key for fairness, global for protection. Put the limiter ahead of the expensive work, keep the 429 path cheap, and size burst from real spike data, not guesses."
